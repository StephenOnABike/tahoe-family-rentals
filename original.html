Snow outlook for Christmas week Updated Nov 15, 2026

What's open around the lake, what's in the forecast, and a realistic outlook for Dec 20–27, focused on South Lake Tahoe and Heavenly.

Bottom line

Tahoe's season is just starting: Heavenly and Northstar plan to open Fri Nov 20, Palisades Nov 25, and Kirkwood Dec 4 [1][4]. Heavenly had only about 6″ of base on Nov 15 [2], but a much bigger storm Wed–Thu could bring 2–3 feet above 7,000 ft and 8–18″ at lake level [7], and forecasters see an active, stormy pattern through Thanksgiving week [6][9]. A strong El Niño is under way [11]; seasonal models lean toward a somewhat slower-than-normal November–December near Tahoe and a bigger January–March [15]. Our read: this fast start makes it likely Heavenly will be running on a real base by Dec 20–27, but how much terrain is open is still a coin flip this far out. The useful picture comes 7–14 days before the trip.

2. The forecast for the next two weeks

  • Sun night–Mon (Nov 15–16): NWS Winter Weather Advisory: 3–7″ above 7,000 ft, highest west of Hwy 89, and up to 1″ at lake level, with ridgetop gusts to 70 mph [7] (Nov 15, 2026).
  • Wed–Thu (Nov 18–19): Winter Storm Watch from 4 am Wed to 4 am Fri. It could bring 2–3 feet above 7,000 ft and 8–18″ around lake level, with ridgetop winds to 80 mph [7] (Nov 15, 2026). NWS calls it a "stronger, colder storm" with "significant Sierra snow." Friday's highs will be 10–15° below normal, a second quick storm is possible late Sat–Sun, and an active pattern looks likely through Thanksgiving week [6] (Nov 15, 2026, 1:46 pm PST).
  • Thanksgiving week (Nov 21–29): NOAA favors above-normal precipitation for the West Coast, with the highest odds (over 50–60%) for northern and central California. The 8–14 day outlook also leans warmer than normal along the coast, so some storms may come with higher snow levels [9] (issued Nov 15, 2026). NOAA flags a moderate risk of heavy snow for the higher northern Sierra on Nov 24–25 from a possible atmospheric river [10] (Nov 15, 2026). OpenSnow's forecaster expects "a break next weekend, with more storms possible Thanksgiving week" [8] (Nov 14, 2026).

3. The big picture: El Niño and NOAA's outlooks

  • El Niño Advisory: the key ocean index (Niño-3.4) reached +2.5°C. NOAA puts the chance of a strong to very strong El Niño through Jan–Mar 2027 at more than 79%, and gives about 60% odds of a historic event [11] (Nov 12, 2026). The next update comes Dec 10.
  • NOAA's official December outlook comes out Thu Nov 19 [12]. Its Dec–Feb maps from Oct 15 (below) leaned wetter and warmer than normal for the Sierra [13].
  • An Oct 17 Chronicle analysis of three seasonal models found about 17% less snowfall than normal near Tahoe in Nov–Dec, then roughly 28% more in Jan–Mar [15] (Oct 17, 2026).

4. A realistic outlook for Dec 20–27

  • History says Christmas is hit or miss: "about a quarter of Christmas seasons are pretty awful" in the Sierra [16] (undated). In the El Niño December of 2023, Heavenly and Palisades had only about a 12″ base on Dec 23, and most resorts ran half their lifts or fewer [17] (Dec 23, 2023).
  • This year is starting stronger: the season's first real storm already hit on Nov 13–14, and another 2–3 feet could fall up high this week [3][7]. Cold air after the storm (Friday highs 10–15° below normal) [6] also helps snowmaking.
  • Our read (not a forecast): expect Heavenly to be open with groomed runs and at least part of the mountain for Christmas week. Full-mountain, powder-day conditions depend on what falls in early to mid December, which nobody can forecast yet. Check the NOAA December outlook (Nov 19) and the forecast about Dec 6–13.

5. Driving: chains and 4WD (Echo Summit / US-50 and I-80)

  • What the signs mean: under R-1, chains are needed unless you have snow tires on at least two drive wheels. Under R-2, the most common level in big storms, chains are needed unless you have 4WD/AWD with snow-tread tires on all four wheels. Under R-3, everyone needs chains. 4WD/AWD vehicles must still carry chains in chain-control areas [18] (page undated).
  • Right now: R-2 is up on US-50 from Twin Bridges to Meyers, the Echo Summit stretch into South Lake [19] (reported Nov 15, 2026, 8:24 pm). On I-80, Caltrans closed eastbound traffic at Colfax on Nov 13 and then reopened it with chain controls over Donner Summit [20] (Nov 13, 2026, updated Nov 14).
  • Practical: rent or bring an AWD/4WD with snow-rated tires, carry chains anyway, and cross Echo Summit or Donner in daylight. Avoid the hours a storm is arriving. Check Caltrans QuickMap or call 1-800-427-7623 before leaving [20].
