Snow outlook for Christmas week

How likely is good snow Dec 20–28, 2026 in Tahoe and Mammoth, compared with the Rockies? Seasonal odds, not a forecast for our dates.

Bottom line

A strong-to-very-strong El Niño tilts California's Sierra toward a wetter-than-normal winter, with the best odds at Mammoth, but forecasters expect the payoff mainly January–March and warn that December may start slow and warm. Christmas in the Sierra is boom or bust: about a quarter of Sierra Christmases are poor, and Mammoth got under 25″ of December snow in 19% of seasons. Mammoth is the safer Sierra bet for Christmas week (highest summit, biggest snowmaking system, strongest El Niño signal), while Tahoe should still have groomed snowmaking runs at Heavenly and Northstar even in a bad year, though terrain may be limited. Book refundable if you can; the real picture comes into focus about 7–14 days out.

Bottom line

  1. The big signal is real. NOAA says a strong-to-very-strong El Niño is underway, with an 83% chance it becomes the strongest on record during Oct–Dec. Every official and expert outlook tilts California's Sierra toward a wetter-than-normal winter, and the best odds sit at Mammoth / central Sierra. Utah and Colorado are closer to a coin flip, apart from southern Colorado and southern Utah. The northern Rockies and the Pacific NW lean warm and dry.
  2. December is the weak part of the signal. OpenSnow, SnowBrains and the Sacramento Bee all expect the Sierra's El Niño payoff mainly January–March. They also say December may start slowly and run warm, with higher snow levels and some rain at lower elevations. The four strongest past El Niños had very different Decembers. Mammoth's December snowfall in those years was 105.5″ (1982), 41.7″ (1997), 71″ (2015), and 12″ (2023).
  3. Christmas in the Sierra is boom or bust, El Niño or not. About a quarter of Sierra Christmases are "pretty awful" (Bestsnow). Mammoth got under 25″ in December in 11 of 57 seasons, or 19% (computed). The Utah Cottonwoods and the snowier Colorado resorts are much steadier at Christmas. For example, Alta averages 96% of its terrain open on Dec 25, and Steamboat 93%.
  4. For your choice: Mammoth is the safer Sierra bet for Christmas week. It has the highest summit, the biggest published snowmaking system in California, and the strongest El Niño signal. Tahoe should still be skiable on groomed snowmaking runs at Heavenly and Northstar even in a bad year, but terrain may be limited. Book refundable if you can, and expect the real picture about 7–14 days out.

1. ENSO: current state and forecast

  • NOAA CPC ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, Oct 8, 2026: El Niño Advisory. "El Niño continues to strengthen, with a strong-to-very strong El Niño likely through January-March 2027 (remaining greater than an 83% chance)." Niño-3.4 is at +2.1 °C, Niño-3 at +3.0 °C, and Niño-1+2 at +3.9 °C. Chance of a historic event (RONI ≥ +2.5 °C, stronger than anything since 1950): 54% for SON, 83% for OND, 70% for NDJ. Next update: Nov 12, 2026.
  • CPC official ENSO probabilities (Oct 2026): El Niño 100% for SON, OND, NDJ, DJF and JFM. La Niña is 0% through winter.
  • CPC seasonal discussion, Oct 15: weekly Niño-3.4 is now +2.4 °C. Models expect a peak around NDJ near +3.0 °C (CPC consolidation) to +3.5 °C (NMME). The QBO is westerly and the PDO negative. That PDO pairing differs from 1982-83, 1997-98 and 2015-16, which all had a positive PDO. CPC judges the PDO's influence "may be minimal" given how strong this El Niño is.
  • What strong El Niño usually means. The Pacific jet shifts south and east, which favors California and the Southwest. The northern tier (PNW, northern Rockies, Montana, BC) runs warmer and drier. Utah and northern/central Colorado sit in between, so their result can go either way.
  • Early-season timing (the key point for your trip):
    • OpenSnow (Aug 11): "historical analogs favor a somewhat slower start in December, followed by a strong signal for above-normal snow mid to late season from January to March" for California. Utah: "not a clear signal… for early season snow (November-December)." Colorado is the exception, with Oct–Dec favored.
    • Sacramento Bee (Sep 1): "don't expect it to come early… hold off the start of snow season past November and possibly even partway into December."
    • Bestsnow (Tony Crocker, historical analysis): El Niño effects are "more probable in the second half of the ski season."
    • Counterpoint: Bryan Allegretto's CSSL analysis (Sep 2023) of 10 significant El Niño seasons near Palisades found December was the strongest month, with a median of 89″, or 156% of the 50-year December median. Those 10 seasons include moderate events, and they do not include 2023-24, whose December was very poor. The evidence on December is mixed.

2. NOAA CPC official outlooks

  • The December 2026 monthly outlook is not out yet. On Oct 15 CPC issued the November 30-day outlook (the 0.5-month lead). The December monthly comes with the next set on Nov 19, 2026, and a final update follows around Nov 30. The NDJ and DJF seasonal outlooks are the closest proxies for now.
  • November 2026 monthly (Oct 15): West Coast precipitation is Equal Chances, because of "disagreement regarding the northward extent of precipitation along the West Coast." Below-normal precipitation is favored for the northern Intermountain West. Temperature tilts above normal along the West Coast and northern tier.
  • NDJ 2026-27 (Oct 15). Read from the map by eye:
    • California: precipitation leans above, about 40–50% at Tahoe and Mammoth. Temperature leans above, about 33–50%.
    • Utah: north is Equal Chances for precipitation, south leans above. Northern Utah temperature leans warm.
    • Colorado: north is Equal Chances for precipitation, south leans above (33–50%). Temperature is mostly Equal Chances.
    • Northern Rockies / Montana: below-normal precipitation favored. Temperature above.
  • DJF 2026-27 (NOAA Winter Outlook press release, Oct 15):
    • Wetter than average is favored "across the southern tier, from the Southwest to the Southeast." The map shows all of California shaded above, with the darkest band (50–60%) over central and southern coastal CA. The Sierra looks to be in the ~40–50% band.
    • Warmer than average is favored for northern California and the PNW. The Southwest, Utah and Colorado are Equal Chances.
    • The Intermountain West is Equal Chances for precipitation, and the northern Mountain West leans dry.
    • On the map, a 33–40% "leaning above" band crosses central Utah and northern Colorado, and most of southern Colorado is 40–50%.
  • NOAA's own caveat: "The outlook does not project seasonal snowfall accumulations as snow forecasts are generally not predictable more than a week in advance."

6. What's on the ground now (mid-October 2026) and water context

  • Tahoe: first dusting on Mon Oct 12, from Tropical Storm Rachel's leftovers, with snow above ~6,500′ (Active NorCal, Oct 12; Allegretto, Oct 13). It is expected to melt quickly as warm, dry weather returns. Another weak system is possible Oct 20–22: OnTheSnow's Oct 16 model pull shows Mammoth 0.5–5″, Heavenly/Kirkwood mostly ≤1″ (up to ~8″ in the wettest run), and mostly rain at Palisades.
  • Mammoth: a Sep 28 dusting at McCoy Station melted. Main Lodge season total is still 0″ (Mammoth's site, OpenSnow).
  • Utah: 8–10″ at Alta and Snowbird by Oct 13. Colorado: first significant storm Oct 14–15, with 6–12″ forecast at many resorts (OpenSnow, Oct 14). Another Rockies storm is possible around Oct 23.
  • October snow says almost nothing about Christmas. It usually melts and does not build a base.
  • Last season (2025-26) was "one of the worst snow seasons on record for the West" (OpenSnow) and very warm. Mammoth got 297″, below its ~350″ stated average.
  • Water / drought:
    • DWR said major reservoirs were at 107% of average on Oct 1, 2026, with Oroville at 85% of average.
    • California Water Watch's Oct 15 "estimated total statewide storage" reads 13.6 MAF / 68% of historical average. That is a different, broader metric, and I could not reconcile the two.
    • The governor declared a state of emergency for El Niño flood readiness.
    • None of this changes the ski odds. It only matters if heavy rain-on-snow events cause road or flood problems, which is a real El Niño risk on Sierra passes such as I-80 and US-50.

Key caveats

  • Seasonal outlooks shift odds; they do not forecast weeks. NOAA won't forecast snowfall more than ~1 week out. OpenSnow and Bestsnow both say decisions are best made 7–10 days ahead.
  • 2015-16 was a "super" El Niño that only came in near average at Tahoe, and 2023-24's December was terrible before a strong finish. Strong El Niño has never guaranteed a good December.
  • Warmth is the main risk for this winter: high snow levels, rain at lower bases (Tahoe resorts' bases sit ~6,200–7,000′), heavy "Sierra cement," and fewer snowmaking windows. Mammoth's elevation helps with all of these.
  • This PDO setup has no clean historical precedent. It is negative, while all past "super" El Niños had a positive PDO. That adds uncertainty in both directions.
  • The CPC map probabilities in this report are my reading of NOAA's color bands, not exact values.
